## 1. Homework & workshop assignments -> [`homework/`](homework/)

**What was the assignment?**

This week was about machine learning. In the workshop we trained our first models with scikit-learn on a breast cancer dataset: KNN, logistic regression and a decision tree. We also learned about overfitting and the confusion matrix. The big assignment was to train at least two models on a real dataset, compare them and look at which mistakes the best model makes. For homework we had to do the same on a second dataset and compare the results. The last part of the notebook was about the Titanic data, as preparation for the hackathon.

**What did I hand in?**

- [`Week5_Workshop_Student.ipynb`](homework/Week5_Workshop_Student.ipynb) - the workshop notebook with the exercises, the big assignment (breast cancer + wine dataset) and the Titanic part.

On the breast cancer data the best models got 96.5 %, but there were still 4 cases where cancer was predicted as benign. On the wine data logistic regression got 100 %, but KNN only 71 %.

**What did I find difficult, and how did I solve it?**

At first I didn't really know what the assignment was about and it was difficult for me to understand it. I had to ask AI many times to explain to me why and how I should do it, but then it was much more clear for me. Also the fact that the computer finds the rule and I don't have to do that was strange, but I feel it makes the work faster, which is good.

**Use of AI tools:** I used Claude to help me with the code and the texts, and to explain the parts I didn't understand. I went through the notebook and ran it myself.

## 4. Reflection

**What is the most important thing I learned this week?**

I've learnt that simple programming is so much different from machine learning. In simple programming I created the rule by myself and put it into the code. I decided to use words like "unsafe" and "danger" and the computer just did what I asked. With machine learning I don't write the rule. I just give many examples with the correct answer and the computer find by itself the rule. Then it use the same rule for new cases.

**Where does this connect to "AI for Good"?**

In my opinion, when it comes to something as serious as cancer, we cannot trust AI 100%. I think AI can be just a helpful tool, but the last word should come from the doctor.
